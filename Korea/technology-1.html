First Jet Wars

The Korean War would be fought mostly with equipment and weapons left over from World War II. However Korea would be the first conflict during which most of the air-to-air fighting involved jets like the swept-wing North American F-86 Sabre versus North Korean MiG-15 fighters designed in the Soviet Union. They fought mainly in an area of southwest Korea nicknamed "MiG Alley." UN pilots were forbidden to cross the border into China because of strict UN rules of engagement. So they fought within a 6,500-square-mile area bordered by the Yellow Sea, Yalun River, and China. The Communist pilots would leave from bases in China right over the border with Korea, fly into Korea, engage the enemy and then return to the safety of the forbidden area.
The Russian MiG -15 debuted on November 15, 1950. With its swept-wing design and British-type jet engine, obtained after a 1946 trade agreement, the MiG design had excellent performance. The American F – 80 Sabre was in short supply but was roughly equivalent to the MiG. When the two jets met in high-speed dogfights, closing in 10 miles in less than 30 seconds, pilot skill was often the only difference. And the United States had the advantage. The American pilots were well-trained, mostly World War II veterans. The North Korean and Chinese pilots had received cursory training in the Soviet Union and had no combat experience. In June 1951, with the utmost secrecy, Soviet fighter units began to be brought in. They were better trained and more aggressive, willing to venture past MiG Alley. These units, however, were on six-week rotations and often just as a pilot became adjusted, he would be returned to Russia. By the end of the war, the Sabres had downed10 MiGs for each Sabre lost.
