The American Expeditionary Force Goes to War

When the United States entered World War I in April 1917, the army was faced with the challenge of mobilization and training 3.8 million men. Because of the desire and need to aid the Allies as quickly as possible, the new soldiers were trained only in basic skills. General Pershing realized that when the units arrived in France they would need much additional training before they could enter combat. The first American units began to arrive in France in June of 1917.

By the fall four divisions of American Doughboys had arrived and were being trained by the French and British. For training in trench warfare, Pershing gratefully accepted the help of the experienced Allies. In October 1917 the first American entered the line to get their baptism of fire. American units were paired with French units and took over portions of the French trench.
