Gas Warfare

In World War I, lethal chemical warfare began on 22 April 1915 when the German Army released chlorine gas at Ypres on the Flanders Front. By the time U.S. soldiers entered combat in 1917, chemical warfare technology was fully developed. New agents, notably phosgene and Mustard, had been developed and used, Cylinders, projectors and artillery shells were the main means of delivery. Gas masks protected soldiers from asphyxiating agents, but no adequate protection had yet been developed against mustard gas when it attacked the skin. Over 25% of all casualties suffered by the AEF were caused by gas. PFC Alfred Evon of Marquette was one such casualties; he was gassed in October 1918 but survived.

The Machine Gun

In 1885 the American Hiram Maxim developed the single barrel machine gun thereby dramatically changing the nature of warfare. The accurate and rapid firing machine gun would give one soldier the fire power of many and contribute to the horrific slaughter of World War I. When the United States entered the war, the U.S. Army had very few machine guns and was dependent on the French Army for most of its light and heavy machine guns.
