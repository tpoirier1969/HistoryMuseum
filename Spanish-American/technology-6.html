In 1896, at the age of 30, Olive Pendill of Marquette chose a nursing career. With two years of training in Chicago at St. Luke’s Hospital, she was prepared to serve in Cuba for her idol, Clara Barton, the founder of the American Red Cross. Years later, Olive wrote of her Spanish-American experiences.

“On Christmas morning it mizzled, there was no dry spot, the wind blew, the stove smoked, the rain fell. The cook deserved our sympathy, although all of us nurses felt sorry for ourselves as we ate a cheerless breakfast of black coffee, cold Boston baked beans and hardtack...”

“We were not allowed to leave camp. Havana was quite forbidden. Besides brawls, there were the dread diseases to be feared that might have been brought back to the hospital, such as malaria and typhoid.”

“Water is brought four miles in barrels. The well water in the near ravine…is forbidden to us.”
