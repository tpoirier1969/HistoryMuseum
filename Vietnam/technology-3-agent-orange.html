Agent Orange

A herbicide developed during World War II was used to clear forests to uncover enemy troops. Operation Ranch Hand sprayed 18-20 million gallons of herbicides, the most common of which was Agent Orange, on Southern Vietnam’s forests, Cambodia, and Laos. The herbicide was applied aerially and by boat, truck, and even backpack. The effects on U.S. troops who handled it and fought in its path include cancer, type 2 diabetes, damage to the nervous system, and severe skin disease. Also, their children may be born with spina bifida. The Department of Veteran’s Affairs pays disability compensation for service-connected illnesses including those caused by Agent Orange. Vietnam vets also filed and won a lawsuit against the makers of Agent Orange. However, many people continue to suffer from its effects.

The harmful dioxin lasts 1-3 years on the surface of the soil and 12 years in the interior. Ecological effects are still visible in Southern Vietnam and the Vietnamese government estimates 400,000 people died of exposure and 500,000 children were born with birth defects.
