Agent Orange

Defoliant / Agent Orange-related Rod Linn

A herbicide developed during World War II was used to clear forests to uncover enemy troops. Operation Ranch Hand sprayed 18–20 million gallons of herbicides, the most common of which was Agent Orange, on Southern Vietnam’s forests, Cambodia, and Laos. The effects on U.S. troops who handled it and fought in its path include cancer, type 2 diabetes, damage to the nervous system, and severe skin disease.

The harmful dioxin lasts 1–3 years on the surface of the soil and 12 years in the interior. Ecological effects are still visible in Southern Vietnam.
