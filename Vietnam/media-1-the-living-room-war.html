The Living Room War

Called the “Living Room War” for the first extensive use of TV cameras.

Some of our most powerful images came from the Vietnam War: a Buddhist monk burning himself alive in protest; a 9-year-old girl running naked and screaming in pain from a napalm attack caused by friendly fire; and the body of a protester at Kent State lying dead after being shot by the National Guard.

Many of these images fueled the anti-war movement. As a result, the U.S. military learned to be much more selective in what images were allowed to reach the public.
