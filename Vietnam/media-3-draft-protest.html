Draft Protest

Protests to the draft have occurred since at least the Spanish American War. During the Vietnam War, draft evasion and resistance reached an all-time peak. Some filed as conscientious objectors, some failed to report for induction, some attempted a disability claim, and some fled to Canada. Because of the high number, President Carter granted amnesty to those who fled the country in 1977.

An estimated one third of the troops in Vietnam were volunteers. Unlike other conflicts, some men volunteered in order to avoid being a “grunt,” or infantryman.

On college campuses, where young men faced the draft following graduation, protests against the draft and the war were the greatest.
