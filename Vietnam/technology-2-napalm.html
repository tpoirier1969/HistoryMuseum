Napalm

While first used during World Wars I and II, it became controversial during its use in Vietnam. It was both a weapon and useful in uncovering enemies by removing foliage. The mixture of fuel and a gelling solution made it stick to most surfaces, including human skin. Major contact on the skin killed nerves, caused third-degree burns, and could result in death. A smaller splash created a painful second-degree burn and survival with severe scars.
