Korea: Axing Incident, August 18, 1976

The Cold War is sometimes called the war in which no bullets were fired. Even without considering Vietnam War and the Korean War, there were many incidents, deaths, injuries and prisoners taken during the Cold War. Here is just one example:'

About 30 North Korean guards (later found to be special hit men) beat two US Army officers to death and wounded several more in the demilitarized zone (DMZ).

The UN Command accounted the incident this way:The UN Command accounted the incident this way:

2. The two American officers, a South Korean officer and several American enlisted men were escorting five Korean service corps workers who were trimming trees in Panmunjom, the truce village in the center of the buffer zone between North and South Korea.

3. Two North Korean officers and several North Korean guards came to the area in midmorning as the South Koreans were “routinely trimming branches” from a tree in the joint security area. The tree was about 35 to 40 yards from a U.N. Command checkpoint at the south side of the Bridge of No Return, over which prison exchanges took place after the Korean Armistice. Scan0010 (bridge)

After some discussion during which one of the North Korean officers expressed objection to the tree trimming, the North Koreans demanded that the South Koreans stop the work.

Shortly after, a North Korean vehicle brought additional guards, and one of the North Korean officers was heard to tell them to “kill” the Americans and South Koreans. The attack followed.

About 45 days were spent in war positions, and a number of “minor” shooting incidents occurred. Marquette resident, John Moschetti (scan0001) was stationed at the DMZ at the time.
