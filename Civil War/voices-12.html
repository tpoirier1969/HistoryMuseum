Speech of a Loyal Indian to the Indians of the 1st Michigan Sharpshooters

Excerpts from The Detroit Advertiser and Tribune, July 11, 1863

The following speech of Nock-ke-chick-jaw-me, Chief of the Chippeways, was delivered to the Indian Company of the 1st Michigan Sharpshooters at Dearbornville, on Saturday by his special interpreter, Rev. H. Jackson, who has been interpreter for the Government.

My children! — I have come a long way from the home of your fathers to see and counsel you; to see you on parade and in camp and learn if you behave like true sons and noble braves. Listen to my words and I will advise you. You must obey the officers set over you; discharge every duty to our country; be loyal to our great father, the President of the United States. Save your money. Your fathers are growing old. They are left behind and will need it — remember them in gratitude for the care and instruction given you.

My children! You are young and inexperienced. I desire you to reflect on your duty. Do not trifle. Understand for what you are fighting; be not boys, but brave and good men. I desire you, my children, to listen to the instruction of your officers; learn your duty and do it, and like your ancestors, you will be heroic and brave. Have confidence in the Great Spirit, and when you die your forefathers will welcome you to the spirit land as brave sons.

As I sat in my wigwam I thought of you — I love you — I am full of joy and gratitude for this privilege of talking to you. I shall visit you and instruct you; and, if necessary, go South to watch and care for you. Do not desert. It will disgrace your people and bring punishment on your hands. Be faithful to your country, brave and valiant, and the Great Spirit will reward you.
