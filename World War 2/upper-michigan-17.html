Conservation and Recycling

A successful collection of furs in Marquette was reported on November 20, 1942 by the Mining Journal. The 300 pounds of furs came from a deceased pet, fur rugs and coats. Some women offered their only winter coat.

Communities across America collected scrap materials to aid in the war effort. The National “Salvage for Victory” program, organized by the Office of Civilian Defense, encouraged all citizens to win the war by conserving food, salvaging materials and buying war bonds. In April of 1942 a “Scrap for Victory” parade was held in Ishpeming. Floats and bands emphasized the need to salvage materials. In Marquette, a Scrap Dance was given for high school students. Admission was 15 pounds of scrap metal. Materials collected in scrap drives included all metals, rubber, basics and cloth, paper and cardboard and fats from meat products. Marquette County miners collected worn out overalls to use for rags.
