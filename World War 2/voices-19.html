Norman “Boots” Kukuk — July 4, 1943...
More Missions... Best fireworks of his life

July 4, 1943

Dear Mom and Dad:

… Yesterday I developed an oil leak in my plane, and had to come 150 miles on ten lbs. oil pressure back to base. I got home with two gallons of oil and when I left the oil tank held eleven gallons. The Line officer said. “Another 20 minutes in the air and your engine would have frozen up completely.”

“So, mother, that is one time your little boy cheated the old man out of crossing my name from his book.”

Today, I took off out of out of gas on my wing tank and had to return to base.

Two days ago a fellow named Ensign Fitzpatrick had to make a forced landing two miles off shore; so when he finally made land in the rubber boat, he camped out over night. When darkness came on, he shot tracer bullets into the air out of his forty-five. Next morning he was picked up by a coast watch who saw tracers in the dark and heard accompanying shots.

Also two days ago eight of our boys took off Grummin wildcats and went over Lunda; they were attacked by thirty-five zeros. One of the boys named Lt. (J.G.) Woods never came back and I believe never will. Five out of the eight came back and they all had a few zeroes. The count was three of ours to nine of theirs.

Probably by the time I send this letter I will have a few zeros to my credits (I’ll soon see).

We had a raid last night and we all crowded down in our fox hole. Only six bombs dropped and they were all near the runway. Not much damage was done.

We have a movie here every night but most of the time we see it in shifts. You know, between one “Condition Red” to another. Maybe in a year we can see some good Jap movies in Tokyo theatres.

Take good care and don’t worry about me, I’m happy and contented. Your loving son and overseas fighter pilot – Boots.

PS. Today was the day for fireworks! We flew C.A.P. over Lunda like I said, and we saw fifteen Jap Bettys (bombers) come in and drop bombs on Rendevo Harbor and ships inside harbor. We were two miles away and 2000 feet lower than they, and all we could do was watch the fight. I saw AA fire hit one bomber squarely and when it blew up, it also caught two more bombers in the explosion. They were on each side and it was fine to see all three go down in flame. But the action became terrific – the other flight of Grummen wildcats and marine F4U’s came in from above and shot down more. Just below us there was a fight and when we went into a dive we saw two zeros flaming and also one Grumman. I did not fire a shot and did not get one bullet hole in plane; but I sure did see a fine show. I made a forced landing back at field with five gallons of gasoline.

I can remember when I was a little boy and went to the fair and saw fireworks, but today was the best of my life. I just couldn’t help thinking that every sky rocket and every flame was ignited to kill human life by human life.

Your wiser and more experienced son who thinks of home more every day.

Boots
