Radio and Fireside Chats

In the late 1920s the radio industry rapidly expanded. By 1939 there were 1,465 radio stations across the U.S. providing Americans with news and entertainment. By the end of World War II, 95% of all homes had a radio.

During the dark days of the Great Depression, President Roosevelt addressed the American people explaining his “New Deal” programs for economic recovery and reassured the people in what became known as FDR’s “Fireside Chats.”

During the war, Roosevelt continued to use the radio to explain the goals and progress of the war and to urge the American people to maintain their support for the war.
