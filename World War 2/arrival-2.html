The G. I. Bill of Rights

In 1944 Congress passed the Serviceman Readjustment Act, commonly called the G.I. Bill of Rights, granting returning World War II veterans a sweeping new array of financial benefits to help them readjust to civilian life. The G.I. Bill guaranteed the loans veterans received from banks for homes, farms and small businesses, provided for job training and helped veterans find jobs and educational benefits. By 1947 1.25 million veterans were enrolled in colleges and universities. The G.I. Bill’s educational programs were some of the most notable social programs our nation has ever offered its veterans. It enabled millions of men and women, most of which could not afford a college education, an opportunity to obtain a degree. This substantially raised the educational level of our citizenry and benefited the entire nation.
