Paratroopers and Glider Troopers

World War II would see the first large scale use of airborne forces using parachutes and gliders to land behind enemy lines to disrupt the enemy’s ability to fight. The U.S. established its first airborne unit in August 1940. From the beginning, U.S. paratroopers exhibited characteristics that remain central to the airborne fighting spirit. All are volunteers, physically and mentally tough, filled with esprit de corps and capable of acting alone in a crisis. The U.S. formed six airborne divisions with parachute and glider units during WWII, and these elite units were in the forefront of many of the most famous and hard fought battles of WWII.

George Keskimaki

George Keskimaki was raised in l’Anse and was a paratrooper in the famous 101st Airborne Division during World War II. After the war he attended Northern Michigan University and then became a biology teach and athletic director at Roseville High School. He went on to tell his story and those of his fellow “Screaming Eagles” in three books about the 101st Airborne Division in WWII.
