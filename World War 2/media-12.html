War Correspondents and Photo Journalists

Newspaper reporters and photographers from the major newspapers and news outlets accompanied the armed forces to every battlefield of the war. Many of these journalists lived with the soldiers and marines sharing the hardships and dangers of the battlefield. The most famous of the war correspondents was Ernest “Ernie” Pyle. Pyle wrote about the common G.I.--how he lived, fought and died. He brought home to the parents of the soldiers the reality of the war and the sacrifices their sons were making. Like many of the soldiers he met and wrote about during the war, Pyle was killed in action on the island of Ie Shima on April 18, 1945.

The experiences of war that Ernie Pyle captured with words, other men, civilian and military photographers and combat cameramen captured on film. These photo journalists provided the footage for the newsreels and photos for hometown newspapers. The most famous photo of World War II, and perhaps of all history, was taken by Joseph J. Rosenthal, of the second flag raising on Mt. Suribachi Iwo Jima on February 23, 1945.
