“Boots” Kukuk — July 4, 1943
Letter continued

Probably by the time I send this letter I will have a few zeros to my credits (I’ll soon see)

We had a raid last night and we all crowded down in our fox hole. Only six bombs dropped and they were all near the runway. Not much damage was done.

We have a movie here every night but most of the time we see it I shifts/ You know between one “Condition Red” to another. Maybe in a year we can see some good Jap movies in Tokyo theatres.

Take good care and don’t worry about me, I’m happy and contented. Your loving son and overseas fighter pilot – Boots

PS. Today was the day for fireworks! We flew C.A.P. over Lunda like I said, and we saw fifteen Jap Bettys (bombers) come in and drop bombs on Rendevo Harbor and ships inside harbor. We were two miles away and 2000 feet lower than they, and all we could do was watch the fight. I saw AA fire hit one bomber squarely and when it flew up, it also caught two more bombers in explosion. They were on each side and it was fine to see all three go down in flame. But the action became terrific – the other flight of Grummen wildcats and marine F4U’s came in from above and shot down more. Just below us there was a fight and when we went into a dive we saw two zeros flaming and also one Grumman. I did not fire a shot and did not get one bullet hole in plane; but I sure did see a fine show. I made a forced landing back at field with five gallons of gasoline.

I can remember when I was a little boy and went to the fair and saw fireworks. but today was the best of my life. I just couldn’t help thinking that every sky rocket and every flame was ignited to kill human life by human life.

Your wiser and more experienced son who thinks of home more very day.

Boots
