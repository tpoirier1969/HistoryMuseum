Mobilizing the Troops, Draftees and Volunteers

With the nation still suffering from the effects of the Great Depression, the Roosevelt administration was hesitant to establish conscription before a declaration of war. But with the defeat of France in June of 1940, the government enacted the nation’s first peacetime draft. 600,000 men were drafted for one year of service. Later in the summer the federal government also mobilized the National Guard and Reserve for one year’s service. In 1941 Congress voted to extend the service of the draftees and with the attack on Pearl Harbor, National Guardsmen, Reservists, volunteers and draftees alike would serve for the duration of the war. After Pearl Harbor Congress extended the draftee age from 18 to 36, and later to 45. 10.1 million men would be drafted and almost six million men and women would enlist. Following the attack on Pearl Harbor there was a rush to enlist.
