Consolidated B-24 Liberator

The B-24 was designed in 1939 and was brought into service with the RAF in June of 1941. The B-24 had a greater operational range than the B-17. It carried a crew of 10: pilot, co-pilot, bombardier, navigator, radio operator, engineer and four gunners. It was powered by four 1,200 hp Pratt and Whitney engines and had a max speed of 290 mph with a ceiling of 28,000 feet and range of 2,200 miles when fully loaded with 8,000 pounds of bombs. It had ten .50 caliber machine guns for defense.

Boeing B-29 Superfortress

The B-29 was the most advanced bomber of the war. Prototypes were ordered in August 1940, and they flew their first combat mission in June of 1944. By early 1945, B-29s would be devastating the Japanese homeland. It would be a B-29, the Enola Gay, which would drop the first atomic bomb on August 6, 1945. It carried a crew of 10 to 14 airmen. It was powered by four 2,200 hp Wright Duplex Cyclone engines and had a max speed of 357 mph with a ceiling of 36,000 feet and range of 3,250 miles when loaded with 10,000 pounds of bombs. It had ten .50 caliber machine guns and 20mm cannons for defense.
