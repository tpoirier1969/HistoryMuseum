Rationing

Rationing was necessary in order to fairly distribute scarce goods such as food, fuel oil and gasoline. Each person, per week was allotted the equivalent of 28 oz. of meat, 7 cups of coffee and one cup of sugar. Drivers could purchase three gallons of gasoline per week for pleasure driving. Business owners, doctors, policemen and farmers could purchase more depending on need.

The Office of Price Administration operated 5,000 ration boards nationwide, staffed by volunteers. Each month, three billion color-coded and numbered ration stamps were issued. Every person, including infants, received ration books. The blue stamps were used to purchase all canned goods. Red stamps allowed the shopper to buy meat, butter and fat.
