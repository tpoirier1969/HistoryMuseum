Morality of Using the Atomic Bomb

In February of 1945 the USAAF began a bombing campaign to destroy the major Japanese cities. On March 9, 325 B-29 bombers using incendiary bombs destroyed more than 16 square miles of Tokyo, killing over 89,000 Japanese. By mid-June, five other large industrial cities, Nagoya, Kobe, Osaka, Yokohama and Kawasaki, had been devastated and 260,000 people killed.

However, the destruction did not deflect the Japanese government from its commitment to continue the war.

“Three years of intense work produced just enough plutonium for two bombs--enough to end the war. On the 50th anniversary of the destruction of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, the media was full of moral debate about the bomb. During the war we were fighting for survival, motivated by fear of what would happen if the Axis powers developed it first. For years afterward, at family visits my cousins thanked me for saving their lives. They’d been stationed in the Pacific, readying for an invasion of Japan that they doubted they’d survive. The proposition that it might be immoral to end the war as quickly as possible did not occur to many of us.”
