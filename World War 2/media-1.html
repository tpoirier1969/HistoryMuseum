World War II - Media and the Message

In the late 1930s and 1940s Americans received their news from three sources - newspaper, radio and newsreels. The often heroic efforts of such men as newspaper columnist Ernie Pyle, radio broadcaster Edward R. Murrow, cartoonist Sgt. Bill Mauldin as well as the combat photographers of the armed forces and civilian media brought the reality of the war to Americans at home.
The government established the Office of War Information to coordinate propaganda activities. The OWI staff met with the media, including heads of Hollywood studios, to suggest political themes the government wanted to promote. The OWI and other agencies that acted as a clearing house for information about the war effort and used a strategy of ‘informational propaganda’ which disseminated information to reassure the public.
