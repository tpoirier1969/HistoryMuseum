Newsreels

The newsreel was a short collection of nonfiction stories lasting from eight to ten minutes that ran before a full-length movie. Fox Movietone, The March of Time, Paramount News, Hearst Metrotone News, and Universal Newsreel were shown in theaters across America, primarily from 1911 to 1967. During World War II, newsreel war-related topics dominated newsreels and served several important functions for civilians: to spread news, to dramatize news, and to unite civilians at home to the cause of the war abroad. According to Paramount News, by 1944 77% of its total content was devoted to war news. While seemingly informational, the real purpose of the newsreels was to dramatize the news. Radio and newspapers continued as the main sources of news while newsreels provided visual spectacle of the same topics. Especially during World War II, airplane delivery took 10-14 days to bring images back to the US.
